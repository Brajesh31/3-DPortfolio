# 🌱 Community Code of Conduct

## Our Commitment

The 3-D Portfolio project welcomes people who want to learn, build, review, improve, and discuss software. This Code of Conduct defines the standards expected in project-managed spaces and explains how concerns can be reported and addressed.

We want project participation to be constructive for people with different backgrounds, experience levels, identities, communication styles, and technical opinions. Good engineering depends on clear disagreement and honest review. It does not require hostility, humiliation, or intimidation.

By participating in this repository, its issues, pull requests, discussions, and related project spaces, participants agree to follow this policy.

## 🧭 Scope

This policy applies to interactions in project spaces, including:

- GitHub issues and issue comments.
- Pull requests and code reviews.
- GitHub Discussions, where enabled.
- Repository documentation and contribution channels.
- Project-related online events or communication where participants are acting on behalf of or in connection with this project.

It also applies to conduct outside these spaces when it is directly connected to project participation and materially affects the safety or ability of someone to contribute.

This policy is not intended to regulate good-faith disagreement about code, design, priorities, or project direction. It addresses conduct that harms or excludes people.

## ✅ Expected Behaviour

Participants are expected to:

### Communicate respectfully
- Use professional, considerate language.
- Critique code, decisions, and ideas rather than attacking people.
- Avoid assumptions about another person's knowledge, intent, or background.
- Give others reasonable time to respond.
- Keep discussion focused on the project or the question being discussed.

### Make feedback useful
- Explain the problem and its impact.
- Provide evidence, reproduction steps, or references when possible.
- Suggest a practical alternative when one is known.
- Distinguish a blocking defect from a preference.
- Acknowledge when a concern has been resolved.

### Support inclusive participation
- Welcome beginner questions without ridicule.
- Avoid discriminatory language and stereotypes.
- Respect names, pronouns, identity, and personal boundaries.
- Avoid unnecessary jargon when a clearer explanation is possible.
- Make a reasonable effort to keep examples and documentation accessible.

### Be honest and responsible
- Represent your own work and other people's contributions accurately.
- Credit sources and contributors where required.
- Do not fabricate test results, approvals, benchmarks, or issue reproductions.
- Do not misrepresent project status or imply that an unmerged proposal is shipped.
- Respect confidentiality and avoid publishing private information.

## 🚫 Unacceptable Behaviour

The following conduct is not acceptable:

- Harassment, discrimination, threats, intimidation, or targeted personal attacks.
- Hate speech or demeaning language directed at protected or personal characteristics.
- Sexual harassment, unwanted sexual attention, or sexualized comments.
- Publishing another person's private information without permission.
- Deliberate humiliation, stalking, or repeated unwanted contact.
- Trolling, sustained bad-faith disruption, or intentional derailment.
- Impersonation, deceptive attribution, or knowingly misleading project communication.
- Threatening violence or encouraging harm.
- Retaliation against someone who reports a concern in good faith.
- Repeatedly ignoring reasonable moderation instructions.
- Deliberately exposing credentials, private messages, or sensitive information.
- Using automated activity to flood discussions or make participation unusable.

A single severe incident may warrant immediate action. Repeated lower-level behaviour may also become unacceptable when it forms a pattern.

## 💬 Technical Disagreement and Review

Strong technical disagreement is allowed and often necessary. Participants may challenge architecture, dependencies, design decisions, implementation quality, performance claims, and project priorities.

A productive technical review should:
1. Identify the relevant code or decision.
2. Explain the concern and its practical consequence.
3. Separate objective correctness issues from subjective preferences.
4. Offer evidence or a reproducible example where possible.
5. Remain open to a reasoned alternative.
6. Avoid personal remarks or assumptions about competence.

Examples:

| Unhelpful feedback | More constructive feedback |
|---|---|
| “This code is terrible.” | “This component repeats the same state update in three places. Could we centralize it to reduce inconsistent behaviour?” |
| “You clearly do not understand React.” | “This effect appears to run after every render because the dependency changes. Here is a reproduction and a possible fix.” |
| “This design is useless.” | “The primary action is difficult to find at mobile widths. Could we test a clearer hierarchy?” |
| “Your PR is pointless.” | “I do not see the user problem this change solves. Can you add the expected behaviour and acceptance criteria?” |

Maintainers may reject a contribution because it is out of scope, technically unsafe, incomplete, or inconsistent with project direction. A respectful explanation is expected, but disagreement with a decision is not itself a conduct violation.

## 📣 Reporting a Concern

If you experience or witness behaviour that violates this policy, contact the repository owner privately through the contact method listed on the owner's GitHub profile.

When reporting:
- Do not start a public argument with the person involved.
- Do not publish private messages or identifying details.
- Share only information needed to understand the concern.
- Include links or timestamps when available.
- State whether there is an immediate safety concern.

For security vulnerabilities, use the private process in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md), not the conduct-reporting route.

## 📝 What to Include in a Report

A useful report may include:

- The project space where the incident occurred.
- A link to the relevant issue, pull request, comment, or discussion.
- A concise description of the behaviour.
- Approximate dates and times.
- Relevant context that helps explain the impact.
- Whether the behaviour has happened before.
- Any immediate safety or privacy concern.
- What outcome or support would help, if you know.

You do not need to investigate the incident yourself or confront the other person before reporting it. Do not gather information through unauthorized access or publish another person's private data.

## 🛠️ Maintainer Responsibilities

Maintainers should:
- Review reports seriously and as impartially as possible.
- Avoid dismissing a report solely because the person reporting it is new or inexperienced.
- Limit access to report details to people who need them to respond.
- Avoid unnecessary public disclosure.
- Consider the impact on everyone involved.
- Apply the policy consistently and proportionately.
- Communicate decisions when doing so does not compromise privacy or safety.
- Document significant enforcement actions appropriately.

A maintainer who is directly involved in a report should, where practical, avoid being the sole decision-maker for that report.

## ⚖️ Enforcement Guidelines

Depending on severity, context, and whether the behaviour is repeated, possible actions include:

1. **Informal correction:** A request to change wording or behaviour.
2. **Formal warning:** A clear statement that the conduct violates this policy.
3. **Content moderation:** Removal or editing of harmful content where the platform permits it.
4. **Temporary restriction:** A pause on commenting, reviewing, or participating in project spaces.
5. **Permanent exclusion:** Blocking or removing access to project spaces for severe or repeated violations.

These are possible responses, not a mandatory sequence. Maintainers may choose a proportionate response based on the circumstances. A severe threat, doxxing incident, or targeted harassment may require immediate restriction.

## 🔒 Confidentiality and Retaliation

Reports should be handled with discretion. Complete confidentiality cannot be guaranteed because investigating a concern may require sharing limited details with relevant people or platform administrators.

Retaliation against a person for making a good-faith report, supporting an investigation, or participating in moderation is prohibited. Retaliation may itself result in enforcement action.

Do not use this policy as a tool to silence reasonable criticism, avoid technical review, or punish someone for disagreeing with a maintainer.

## 📨 Appeals and Clarification

If someone believes an enforcement decision was based on materially incorrect information, they may contact the repository owner privately with a concise explanation and relevant evidence.

An appeal is not a guarantee that a decision will be reversed. The project may decline repeated appeals that do not provide new information or that are used to continue disruptive conduct.
